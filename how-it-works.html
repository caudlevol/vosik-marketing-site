How it works

Put the terms you negotiated back into the buying decision.

Vosik helps your purchasing team turn documents and market information into findings it can review—and action it can take.

01 / BRING THE DOCUMENTS

Start with the facts.

Upload the purchasing documents and agreements your team already works with. Guided onboarding helps your people get started.

02 / EXAMINE THE FINDINGS

Find what deserves a closer look.

Review potential discrepancies, relevant price movements, and rebate opportunities in the context of your purchases.

03 / TAKE ACTION

Put the work into the decision.

Review the recommended next step, edit the prepared supplier response, and track follow-up. Your team stays in control.

01

Supplier increases

A price-increase letter is a request. Check it.

Vosik checks each letter against your supplier agreements — price caps, index ties, notice periods, national-versus-local scope, reopener clauses — and relevant market information, then gives your buyer a verdict and a counter position to consider.

Vosik prepares a response. Your buyer reviews, edits, and sends it.

02

Tariffs & market changes

The market moved. Your buying decision should account for it.

Bring relevant tariff information and commodity movements into your review. Examine the basis for an increase and whether buying timing deserves another look.

Ask what changed, what applies to your materials, and what it means for the next order.

03

Rebates

A rebate you forget to claim doesn’t help the budget.

Review purchasing records and agreements for potential rebates, track your position against tiers and per-home agreements, and get warned before a rebate is forfeited. Keep deadlines and follow-up visible so opportunities do not get buried in other work.

Your team confirms eligibility and completes the steps required to claim.

04

Bid comparisons

Find the differences before they become costs.

Compare bids, units, and rebate terms more clearly — including lump-sum turnkey quotes leveled against unbundled bids without invented unit conversions. Reduce the manual work needed to put competing offers on a useful basis.

Spend your time evaluating the decision, not rebuilding the comparison.

05

Savings & reporting

Show finance what actually happened.

Prepare purchasing reports and track savings opportunities through follow-up — including what was paid versus what the contract says, and purchasing cost per home closed. Separate what has been identified from what has been acted on and confirmed.

An opportunity is not a verified saving. Keep the distinction clear.

Practical questions

Know what your team is getting.

What does our team need to provide?

Start with relevant purchasing records, supplier agreements, price-increase letters, and bid documents. Confirm document requirements and coverage with Sales for the workflow you want to evaluate.

Does Vosik send supplier emails for us?

Vosik prepares a response for your buyer to review, edit, and send. Your team controls the decision and the supplier relationship.

Will our team receive training?

Guided document uploads, training, and monthly review calls help your team use the tools. Confirm the onboarding and support scope in your proposal.

How should we measure the value?

Choose a recurring purchasing task. Measure the manual baseline, the work required with Vosik, and any financial results your team can verify. Published time comparisons are founder estimates, not measured customer results.

Is the scope the same for every company?

Confirm users, purchasing departments, document needs, and workflows with Sales. Your proposal should make the included scope clear.
